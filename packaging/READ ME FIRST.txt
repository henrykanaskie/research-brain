research brain: installing it
=============================

You don't need Python, GitHub or anything else. You need about 700 MB of free space for the install
(plus room for any PDFs you keep on this computer) and an internet connection for the install itself.

MAC
1. Double-click the zip if you haven't yet, then open the folder it makes.
2. Double-click "Install-research-brain.command".
3. If the Mac says it "can't be opened" or "Apple could not verify" it: open System Settings >
   Privacy & Security, scroll down, click "Open Anyway" next to Install-research-brain.command,
   then double-click it again.
   Still blocked? Open Terminal (Applications > Utilities), type  bash  and a space, drag
   Install-research-brain.command into the Terminal window, and press Return.
4. A window shows the install (a few minutes the first time). When it says Done, research brain
   opens. From then on, open it from Launchpad or your Applications folder.

WINDOWS
1. Unzip it first: right-click research-brain-installer-....zip and choose "Extract All...", then
   Extract. (Double-clicking the zip only shows what's inside it; the installer won't run from there.)
2. In the folder that opens, double-click "Install-research-brain.cmd".
3. If Windows says "Windows protected your PC": click "More info", then "Run anyway".
4. When it says Done, research brain opens. From then on, open it from the Start menu.
   On a university-managed PC, an antivirus or IT policy can block installers like this one. If it
   stops with an error, take a screenshot of the window (see REPORTING A PROBLEM below).

AFTERWARDS
- You can delete this folder (and the zip it came in). Your library lives in Documents > research brain.
- AI (optional): asking questions about your papers, critiques and explanations use an AI you set up
  yourself. The free option is Gemini. In research brain, click the AI name at the top of the window,
  or see README.md, "Add an AI". Everything else works without one.
- Chrome extension (optional): unzip research-brain-extension-....zip into a folder you keep that is
  NOT in OneDrive or another synced folder (for example C:\research brain extension, or a folder in
  your home folder), then in Chrome go to chrome://extensions, turn on Developer mode, click
  "Load unpacked" and choose that folder. (A university-managed Chrome may not allow Developer mode.)
- Already keep your PDFs in folders (OneDrive too)? In research brain: Add > Add folders of papers.
  They stay where they are (nothing is copied), and each folder becomes a topic.
- Short on disk space? Keep your PDFs in OneDrive: README.md, "Short on disk space?".
- Updates: when there's a newer version, download it, unzip it and run its installer again, the
  same way. If research brain is open, the installer closes it first. Your library stays exactly
  as it is.
- Everything else (adding papers, free paper-lookup keys, a first-ten-minutes tour) is in README.md
  in this folder: open it with any text editor or a browser.

REPORTING A PROBLEM
Include what you did, what you saw (a screenshot helps), your research brain version (Library settings,
or the installer window), and the end of the log:
  Mac:     ~/Library/Application Support/research_brain/app.log
  Windows: paste  %APPDATA%\research_brain  into File Explorer's address bar, open app.log
Never send API keys or passwords.
